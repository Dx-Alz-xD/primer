How it works

Four steps from a passing curiosity to a course you can finish.

  1. 1

    Name your topic

    Add a range, like “from limits to the chain rule”, and say how you like to be taught.

  2. 2

    AI plans the lessons

    You get a detailed outline of 20 to 30 lessons, grouped into units, in the order to learn them.

  3. 3

    Learn in checkpoints

    Each lesson opens piece by piece as you answer. Get one wrong and it revises with you first.

  4. 4

    Ask about anything

    Select a line or sketch on the whiteboard to ask a doubt right where it came up.

Built for how you actually learn

Reading is only half of it. These are the parts that make it stick.

  • Set the range

    Start from the very beginning or from what you already know, and stop where you need to.

  • Teaching instructions

    Ask for LaTeX, mnemonics, worked examples or “explain like I’m five”, and the lessons follow.

  • Checkpoint lessons

    Questions sit inside the lesson. The next part opens only when you’ve answered.

  • Revision when you slip

    A wrong answer brings back the key points of that section before you try again.

  • Real maths notation

    Formulas render properly, so fractions and roots look the way they do in a textbook.

  • Ask about any line

    Highlight a sentence and ask about exactly that part, without leaving the lesson.

  • Whiteboard doubts

    Circle, underline or sketch on the notes, then ask about what you drew.

  • Written answers

    Some questions want your own words, with a model answer to compare against.
